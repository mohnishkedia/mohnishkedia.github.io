About

I am a Research Fellow at the Institute for Digital Medicine (WisDM), Yong Loo Lin School of Medicine, National University of Singapore, and completed my PhD in Public Policy at NUS in 2026. My doctoral thesis, Making Collaborations Work — Understanding the Role of Policy Capacity, was supervised by Professor M Ramesh at the Lee Kuan Yew School of Public Policy.

My training spans public policy, economics, and China studies, with degrees from NUS, Peking University, the University of Cambridge, and the University of Delhi, and a year of doctoral research at Yale University as a Fox International Fellow. I think in the shoes of government: connecting policy goals with the right instruments and aligning NGOs, business, and the state so that public benefit is maximised.

Research Interests

Policy Capacity & Collaboration

What organisational, analytical, and political capacities allow cross-sector collaborations to be sustained rather than stall after promising starts.

Digital Medicine Adoption

Diagnosing the validation-to-adoption gap ("pilotitis") in digital therapeutics and AI-guided care through an implementation and governance lens.

Public-Private Partnerships

Designing durable PPP architectures — accountability, governance, and equity safeguards — that align public stewardship with private innovation.

Comparative Health Systems

Regulatory, reimbursement, and human-resource policy across Asian systems, with particular depth on China and India.

Anticipatory Policy Design

Futures thinking and proactive resilience — how organisations and governments prepare for uncertainty before it arrives.

Sustainability Governance

Coalitions, nonprofit readiness, and collaborative environmental restoration in strong-state contexts.
